# Copywriting Style Guide

This style guide ensures all programmatic SEO content sounds human, professional, and consistent.

---

## Core Writing Rules

### 1. Language Preferences

**British English** (default - adjust for your market):
- optimise, not optimize
- realise, not realize
- colour, not color
- behaviour, not behavior
- organisation, not organization
- centre, not center
- programme (but "program" for software)

### 2. Punctuation

**No em dashes.** Use periods or commas instead.

❌ Bad: "Guests upload photos—which appear instantly—on the big screen."  
✅ Good: "Guests upload photos. They appear instantly on the big screen."

**No exclamation marks** (except sparingly in CTAs if absolutely needed).

❌ Bad: "Start your event today!"  
✅ Good: "Start your event today."

### 3. Paragraph Structure

**Short paragraphs: 1-3 sentences maximum.**

❌ Bad:
```
Traditional methods of collecting photos from events have always been problematic. 
People promise to email their photos but rarely follow through, and when they do, 
the files are often too large to send via email, or they end up in spam folders, 
or the recipients simply forget to download them before the links expire, which 
means that despite everyone's best intentions, most of those precious moments 
captured by guests are lost forever.
```

✅ Good:
```
Traditional photo collection rarely works. People promise to email photos but never do.

When they do send them, files are too large. Links expire. Downloads get forgotten.

Most guest photos are lost forever.
```

### 4. Sentence Length

**Short, punchy sentences. Vary length for natural rhythm.**

Mix:
- Very short. (2-4 words)
- Short sentences that make a point. (5-8 words)
- Medium sentences that provide context or explanation. (10-15 words)

❌ Bad: "The platform allows users to upload their photos and videos through a simple QR code scanning process that doesn't require any app downloads."

✅ Good: "Guests scan a QR code. They upload photos and videos instantly. No app download required."

## Tone Guidelines

### Be:

| Quality | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Confident** | State facts directly, not tentatively |
| **Helpful** | Focus on solving the reader's problem |
| **Professional** | Clear, competent language |
| **Conversational** | Natural phrasing, not stiff |
| **Human** | Real voice, not robotic |

### Avoid Being:

| Quality | Example to Avoid |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Arrogant** | "The only solution you'll ever need" |
| **Patronising** | "You might be wondering how this works..." |
| **Stiff** | "The system facilitates the aggregation of..." |
| **Casual** | "Super easy and totally awesome!" |
| **Trying too hard** | "We're absolutely thrilled to..." |

## Words to Avoid

### AI-Tell Words

These words immediately signal AI-generated content. **Never use them:**

```
delve           unlock          harness
seamlessly      embark          revolutionise
game-changer    empower         transform
cutting-edge    leverage        synergy
holistic        paradigm        ecosystem
innovative      robust          scalable
streamline      elevate         curate
```

### Marketing Fluff

Avoid empty superlatives and hype:

```
amazing         incredible      powerful
ultimate        best-in-class   world-class
revolutionary   breakthrough    next-generation
stunning        beautiful       gorgeous
```

If something is genuinely good, explain why with specifics instead.

❌ Bad: "Our revolutionary platform delivers stunning results."  
✅ Good: "The platform collects 3x more photos than traditional methods."

### Weak Fillers

Remove these unless grammatically necessary:

```
very            really          just
actually        basically       literally
essentially     simply          truly
```

## Sentence Patterns

### Lead with Benefit, Follow with Feature

❌ Bad: "Our QR code system means guests don't need to download apps."  
✅ Good: "Guests skip app downloads. They scan a QR code and upload directly."

### State, Don't Ask

❌ Bad: "Struggling to collect photos from your event guests?"  
✅ Good: "Guest photos often scatter and get lost after events."

### Show, Don't Tell

❌ Bad: "Our platform is very easy to use."  
✅ Good: "Guests scan a code and upload in under 10 seconds."

### Be Specific

❌ Bad: "Save time on photo collection."  
✅ Good: "Photo collection takes minutes, not weeks of follow-up."

## Section-Specific Guidelines

### Headlines (H1, H2, H3)

- **No questions in headlines** (sounds uncertain)
- **No colons in H1** (keyword should flow naturally)
- **H1 = exact keyword**, title-cased
- **H2/H3 = variations or descriptive**

❌ Bad H1: "Wedding Photo Challenges: The Platform You Need"  
✅ Good H1: "Wedding Photo Challenges Platform"

❌ Bad H2: "Why Choose Our Platform?"  
✅ Good H2: "How It Works"

### Opening Paragraphs

Start with the problem or context. No "Imagine..." or "What if..." openings.

❌ Bad: "Imagine a world where all your wedding photos appear in one place instantly..."  
✅ Good: "Guest photos from weddings scatter across dozens of devices. Most never get shared."

### Benefit Descriptions

Be concrete, not abstract. Include specifics.

❌ Bad: "Improved engagement and better outcomes for your event."  
✅ Good: "Challenges give guests something fun to do. They mingle more and take creative photos."

### CTAs

Direct and specific. One primary action.

❌ Bad: "Ready to revolutionise your event experience? Get started now!"  
✅ Good: "Start your event." → [Try {Product} for your wedding]

## Examples: Good vs Bad

### Example 1: Opening

**Bad:**
> Imagine a world where your team seamlessly harnesses the power of automation to unlock unprecedented productivity gains! Our cutting-edge platform empowers you to effortlessly transform your business processes.

**Good:**
> Automated workflows handle repetitive tasks. Your team gains three hours per week for strategic work.

### Example 2: Feature Description

**Bad:**
> We're excited to share our revolutionary platform that empowers you to leverage innovative technology for streamlined photo collection that truly transforms the event experience.

**Good:**
> The platform connects to any screen. Guests upload photos via QR code. Photos display automatically.

### Example 3: Benefit

**Bad:**
> Unlock the power of real-time engagement to create truly unforgettable moments that your guests will cherish forever!

**Good:**
> Photos appear on screens within seconds of upload. Guests see their photos displayed and upload more.

### Example 4: CTA

**Bad:**
> Ready to embark on your journey to event excellence? Don't wait—revolutionise your gatherings today with our game-changing solution!

**Good:**
> Start collecting photos at your next event. → [Start your event]

|-------------|----------|

| Cutting-edge innovation that disrupts... | New method that reduces setup time by 60% |
| Our solution empowers teams to... | Teams can now... |

## Formatting Guidelines

### Headings
- H1: Page title (one per page)
- H2: Major sections
- H3: Subsections and benefit titles
- H4+: Rarely needed in SEO pages

### Lists
- Use bullet points for features and benefits
- Keep lists to 3-5 items
- Short intro sentence before lists
- Consistent structure within lists

### Emphasis
- **Bold** for key terms (sparingly)
- Avoid ALL CAPS except acronyms
- No underlining (looks like links)
- No italic for emphasis (reserve for titles)

### Numbers
- Use numerals for stats: "3x more photos", "10 seconds"
- Spell out small numbers in prose: "three benefits"
- Use numerals for lists: "1. First step"

## SEO Writing Guidelines

### Keyword Integration

**Natural placement, not forced:**

❌ Bad (keyword stuffing):
> Wedding photo challenges platform is the best wedding photo challenges platform for wedding photo challenges at weddings.

✅ Good (natural):
> Wedding Photo Challenges Platform

> Photo challenges turn passive wedding guests into active participants. Guests compete through missions, uploading photos as they complete each task.

### Keyword Placement

1. **H1:** Exact keyword (required)
2. **First paragraph:** Keyword or close variation (required)
3. **H2/H3:** Variations (natural placement)
4. **Body text:** Semantic variations (natural placement)
5. **Meta title:** Exact keyword + brand
6. **Meta description:** Keyword + benefit

### Meta Descriptions

- 150-160 characters
- Include primary keyword near start
- Include call-to-action or benefit
- No duplicate descriptions across pages

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing, verify each page:

- [ ] 400-500 words total
- [ ] British English spelling (or your preference)
- [ ] No em dashes
- [ ] No exclamation marks
- [ ] No AI-tell words
- [ ] Paragraphs 1-3 sentences
- [ ] Varied sentence lengths
- [ ] H1 uses exact keyword
- [ ] No questions in headlines
- [ ] Concrete benefits, not abstract
- [ ] Specific examples included
- [ ] Natural, human cadence
- [ ] Professional but not stiff

---

## Reading Aloud Test

**The ultimate quality check:** Read the content aloud.

If it sounds:
- Natural → ✅ Good
- Robotic → ❌ Rewrite
- Like marketing → ❌ Tone down
- Like AI → ❌ Humanise

If you stumble on a sentence, it needs rewriting.

---

## Remember

> The reader is evaluating, not dreaming. Give them facts, clear explanations, and proof. Skip the theatre.

Write as if you're explaining to a colleague who needs to understand your product quickly. Be helpful, be clear, be human.
